Nintendo Hard

When arcade-style difficulty came home
Name:Class:Date:

1. Do Now

You lose a game in one minute. Would you feel differently if you paid for that attempt or already owned the game?

Write 1–2 sentences. Explain what would make you try again.

2. Arcade ↔ NES: discuss, then share

Which arcade-style difficulty rules carried over to the NES? Did those rules still fit playing at home?

Talk with the person next to you. Be ready to share one observed rule with the class. No written answer is required here.

3. NES investigation

Game:  

Record one piece of evidence.

Keep it brief: “When I ___, the game ___. On my next attempt, I ___.”

Which part of your game created a useful challenge, and which part made you want to stop?

After recording your evidence, discuss the question with your partner. Then share an example with the class.

4. Change one rule

Choose a game you investigated. Change one rule to make it a better fit for a new home player.

“In [game], I would change ___ to ___. This would help a new player because ___. It would still be challenging because ___.”

Does a game need to become easier when it moves into someone’s home?

After your redesign, discuss this question with your partner. Be ready to explain your decision to the class.

5. Exit ticket

Name one arcade-style difficulty rule in a game from today. Explain why keeping that rule made sense—or needed rethinking—when people played at home.

Name the game, describe the rule, and explain its effect. Use evidence rather than only “it was hard.”

Tomorrow: games over time

Come in thinking about the different platforms you play on—or have seen older games played on. What differences do you notice in how those games look, sound, and work?

No written response needed. Bring an example to talk about tomorrow.
